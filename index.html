About

I'm a GRC Analyst at Deloitte USI working in Third-Party Risk Management — running vendor onboarding intake calls, managing periodic risk reassessments on IBM OpenPages, monitoring third-party risk signals through Interos and RapidRatings, and participating in control testing. My background sits at an uncommon intersection: Big 4 TPRM experience, a technical security foundation from hands-on work with OWASP, Burp Suite, and Linux, and real AI governance practice from validating an AI tool's automated SOC 2 control extraction against AICPA Trust Services Criteria — benchmarking AI output against human-reviewer judgment.

That last project is what I'm most proud of. Subservice org identification, carve-out handling, framework accuracy — that's AI governance in practice, not as a concept. It's also what pushed me to go deeper: I completed an 11-week self-directed GRC Mastery Program applied to a simulated fintech (PaySphere), producing 10 portfolio deliverables across ISO 27001, NIST CSF 2.0, SOC 2, AI governance, vendor risk, and regulatory compliance.

I'm building toward internal GRC ownership at a product or technology company — designing risk programs end-to-end, not executing pieces of someone else's. The PaySphere simulation below is the proof of that capability.

How I Got Here

2021 — Third Year of B.Tech

The incident that changed my direction

I clicked a link. Lost access to my phone. My data was out there and I had no idea how it happened or how to respond. That experience hit differently when you're a CS student who thought they understood how technology worked. It made the abstract — data exposure, social engineering, access control — suddenly very real and very personal. I started pulling threads: YouTube deep-dives, security blogs, anything that explained how attacks actually worked.

2022–2023 — Building the Foundation

From curiosity to hands-on practice

Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate gave me the structured base. Then I went practical — Burp Suite for web application testing, Linux environments, OWASP Top 10 in depth, CTF challenges. I wasn't chasing a specific job title yet; I just wanted to understand how systems actually break. That technical grounding later became one of my clearest differentiators in GRC — being able to read a penetration test report and understand what it means operationally, not just flag it as a finding.

2024 — Joining Deloitte

Campus placement, then a deliberate move

Joined Deloitte through campus recruitment into QA and content operations on Adobe Experience Manager — structured testing, defect documentation, access logic in YAML. Useful grounding in SDLC and how large organizations handle system quality. But my interest was clearly in the risk and governance side. I spent those months building toward an internal move — studied GRC frameworks, completed the ISO 27001 Lead Auditor certification, and went through the internal interview process into the Cyber GRC practice.

2025 — Present

GRC in practice, AI governance in real use

Now running TPRM at Deloitte — vendor intake, risk reassessments, control testing, third-party signal monitoring. The AI governance project came through real work: validating an in-development AI tool's SOC 2 control extraction against AICPA Trust Services Criteria. That project crystallised what I want to build next: an internal GRC function at a product company where I own the program, not just execute parts of it. The 11-week PaySphere simulation is me proving I can do exactly that.
